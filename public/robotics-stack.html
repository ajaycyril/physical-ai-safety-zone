02 / POLICY ROUTER

The moat is not choosing a model. It is choosing correctly every time.

Task semantics

Navigation, inspection, manipulation, perception, dialogue, recovery and anomaly handling require different intelligence.

WHAT KIND OF PROBLEM?

Embodiment fit

Policies are only eligible when they support the robot morphology, sensors, action space and calibrated control interface.

CAN THIS MODEL DRIVE THIS BODY?

Risk + authority

High-risk actions may require deterministic planners, tighter constraints or human approval regardless of model confidence.

WHAT IS ALLOWED?

Latency + connectivity

Cloud reasoning can assist planning, but reactive safety and core motion must survive network loss.

WHERE MUST IT RUN?

Confidence + fallback

If a policy is uncertain or drifts outside its operating envelope, route to a safer model, classical skill or remote operator.

WHEN DO WE SWITCH?

Economics

Use the cheapest intelligence that meets success and safety thresholds rather than invoking frontier models for every step.

04 / CROSS-CUTTING PLANES

Two planes make the stack deployable at enterprise scale.

Data / Learning Plane

  • TELEOPCapture expert interventions and recovery demonstrations.
  • EPISODESStore successful, failed and edge-case trajectories with context.
  • SIMULATIONReplay and test mission logic before physical deployment.
  • SYNTHETICGenerate rare scenarios and expand environment diversity.
  • EVALUATIONBenchmark policies by task success, safety, latency and intervention rate.
  • FINE-TUNINGAdapt selected models only where proprietary data creates advantage.

Safety / Trust Plane

  • IDENTITYRobot, user, service and site identities are cryptographically bound.
  • PERMISSIONSCapabilities are scoped by role, site, mission and risk class.
  • GEOFENCINGSpatial policies constrain where robots and skills may operate.
  • APPROVALHuman authority gates material, novel or high-risk actions.
  • AUDITEvery decision, command, override and outcome is replayable.
  • FAIL-SAFEIndependent stop, recovery and degraded-mode behavior.
